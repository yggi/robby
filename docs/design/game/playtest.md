# Playtest — what happened when a child played it

**Exploratory.** One child, one long session. She is also the child the game is
dedicated to, which makes this the strongest evidence available and the least
generalisable at the same time. What it says about *how she solved things* is
data. What it says about children is a hypothesis.

Emilia, five.

## She solves every level in the game on her own

All four authored worlds, unassisted.

The thing worth writing down is *how*: **she makes the arrow shapes with her
hand as she reasons**, tracing the direction in the air before she reaches for
the token. Nobody taught her that and nothing in the game asks for it.

Three things follow.

**The gap between plan and execution landed.** She is not driving the robot; she
is rehearsing a plan away from the board and then committing it. That is the
entire thesis ([`thesis.md`](thesis.md)), and it arrived without a word of text.

**The direction colours are doing their job.** The gesture is a *direction*, not
a symbol lookup — she is not decoding an arrow glyph, she is recalling a way to
go and matching it under her thumb. That is the argument for canonical direction
colours running through the tray token, the filled slot and the planning vector
([`../feel/visual-language.md`](../feel/visual-language.md)), and it is now
evidence rather than an argument.

**Three tuning numbers were guesses that came out right.** The 380ms step, the
bonk shudder and one-tap slot removal all survived contact with the only user
who matters. None of them needs revisiting on current evidence.

## The editor is the runaway success

It was shipped as "a first slice". She loves it, and it turned the game into
something played *between* people: she and her father build rooms for each other
to solve. An adult building with belts and bridges can make rooms that are a
real challenge for older children, so the ceiling is far higher than the shipped
content suggests.

**This reordered the whole board.** The editor's missing pieces — gates and
plates, one-ways, keys, multi-part goals, a grid that is not fixed at 9×7 — are
no longer a rough edge on a side feature. They are the constraint on the part of
this that people actually use. Sharing a built room stops being a neat idea
("the map is `string[]`, so a room is a short URL") and becomes the obvious next
feature, because rooms are *already* being passed between people by hand.

## What this session did not settle

**The beginning.** Emilia had an adult beside her the first time. Whether a
child meets Robby cold and gets moving is a different question from whether the
game is solvable, and it is the one to watch next — `docs/BOARD.md` [R-009], and the
thread in `docs/NOTES.md`.
